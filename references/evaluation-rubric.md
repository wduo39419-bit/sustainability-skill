# Sustainability Skill evaluation rubric

## Minimum input schema

- Technology and intended output
- Application, customer, geography, and deployment scale
- Baseline alternative and functional unit
- Maturity stage and available validation data
- Capacity, utilization, lifetime, and ramp-up assumptions
- CAPEX, OPEX, price, energy, feedstock, labor, and financing inputs
- Emissions factors, energy mix, transport, and end-of-life assumptions
- Relevant permits, standards, incentives, and carbon-price assumptions

If a field is unavailable, mark it as missing and rate the impact on the conclusion. Do not silently substitute a favorable value.

## Suggested indicator set

| Dimension | Core indicators | Typical evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Green value | kg CO2e/output, avoided CO2e, energy intensity, water intensity, material intensity, waste, abatement cost | LCA, measured energy data, supplier data, independent verification |
| Profitability | revenue/unit, variable cost/unit, gross margin, break-even, CAPEX, payback, NPV, IRR, cash-flow minimum | audited accounts, pilot cost records, contracts, quotations, financial model |
| Feasibility | maturity, uptime, yield, scale-up factor, supply security, permit status, safety, customer/offtake evidence | operating logs, test reports, permits, supplier commitments, signed offtake |

## Scenario rules

Use at least three cases:

- Conservative: lower utilization/yield/price, higher CAPEX/OPEX, slower ramp-up, and weaker policy support.
- Base: most defensible values supported by current evidence.
- Optimistic: plausible upside only; never use an aspirational target as the base case.

Report the variables that flip the decision. A good sensitivity result names thresholds, for example: “Base-case NPV becomes negative when utilization falls below 62%.”

## Confidence rubric

- High: multiple independent, recent, context-matched sources or operating data.
- Medium: credible technical or pilot evidence, but limited scale, geography, or independent verification.
- Low: laboratory results, vendor claims, analogy-based estimates, or major missing inputs.

Confidence is assigned separately for carbon, economics, and feasibility. The overall confidence should not exceed the weakest decision-critical dimension.

## Fatal-blocker checklist

Check explicitly for unresolved:

- safety or environmental hazards;
- inability to meet a required standard or permit;
- unavailable critical feedstock, equipment, or utility;
- unproven scale-up mechanism;
- economics that fail under the base case and credible financing assumptions;
- dependence on temporary support without a path to unsubsidized viability;
- emissions benefits that rely on double counting or an invalid baseline.
